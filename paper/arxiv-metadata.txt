Title: The stacking number of a tree
Authors: John Fairfax-Ball
Abstract:
Csern\'ak and Soukup introduced the stacking number of a graph: the least integer $t\ge2$ such that every configuration of $t$ pebbles can be transformed by pebbling moves into a configuration supported on one vertex. They conjectured an explicit formula for the stacking number of a tree in terms of the degrees of its vertices and their distances from a chosen root. We prove this conjecture for every finite tree with at least two vertices. We also show that whether a configuration on a tree can be stacked at a given vertex is decided exactly by the sign of an integer score built from recursive branch messages; consequently, stackability can be tested with a linear number of arithmetic operations. The proof combines this criterion with an explicit zero-score obstruction and a weighted cancellation argument for arbitrary nonstackable configurations. The main theorem and the stackability criterion are formalized in Lean 4, and the main theorem has passed Palomar mechanical verification; it is publicly registered as PALOMAR-2026-09-25-000010.
